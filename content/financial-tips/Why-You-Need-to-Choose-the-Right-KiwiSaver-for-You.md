Working out whether your fund is the best one for you only takes a few minutes and it’s much easier to switch providers than most people think.

This article covers the four key factors that will help you decide if your KiwiSaver fund is a good fit and what it can cost you to stay in the wrong one.

We’ll also share a quick self-check you can carry out against your latest statement, outline how switching works and discuss why it’s a good idea to compare your fund with others across the entire market.

**Key takeaways:**

* Your KiwiSaver fund should match your goals, timeline, comfort with risk and values.
* If you never actively chose your fund, you're likely in a default one that’s not personalised to you.
* Since 1 April 2026, the default contribution rate is 3.5% (rising to 4% from 1 April 2028), so it’s a good time to check your fund, not just your rate.
* Switching funds or providers usually takes a few weeks, and your balance moves with you. Nothing is withdrawn or taxed.

## What to Consider When Assessing Your KiwiSaver Fund

Your KiwiSaver fund is right for you when it matches your goals, timeline, comfort with risk and values. If any of these is misaligned, a fund that's working well for your workmate might be the wrong one for you.

### Goals

Are you saving towards buying your first home in the next few years or building a retirement fund you won't touch for decades? These two common KiwiSaver goals usually suit different types of funds.

### Timeline

The amount of risk you can comfortably take on depends on when you need the money. If you’re a decade or more away from withdrawing your funds, riding out a few bumps in the market is usually worthwhile. But when you’re a year or two out, a sharp drop right before you need to withdraw is problematic.

### Risk comfort

Two people with the same timeline can have different tolerances for watching their balance dip in a downturn. A fund that keeps you up at night isn't the right one, even if the numbers say it should be.

### Values

You might not want your KiwiSaver invested in fossil fuels, weapons or tobacco. Ethical fund options can let you avoid that.

## What Staying in the Wrong Fund Actually Costs You

Staying in the wrong KiwiSaver fund can cost you the growth or safety you should have had over years that you don't get back.

If you never actively chose a fund, you're probably sitting in a default one. According to the [Financial Markets Authority](https://www.fma.govt.nz/consumer/kiwisaver-and-superannuation/about-kiwisaver/kiwisaver-default-funds/), default funds are assigned automatically to anyone who hasn’t made a specific choice. They’re balanced funds, meaning they offer a reasonable middle ground for someone who hasn't decided, but they’re not personalised for anyone in particular.

As an example, picture two 25-year-olds with 40 years until retirement. One stays in a default balanced fund, whereas the other actively chooses a growth fund that’s better suited to a long timeline. Over four decades, the default option can lead to a noticeably smaller retirement balance because the fund type never matched the timeframe.

The reverse scenario is risky as well. If you’re planning to withdraw for a first home next year, a growth or aggressive fund is probably too volatile right now.

With the [default contribution rate increasing to 3.5% on 1 April 2026](https://www.ird.govt.nz/kiwisaver-changes) and a further rise to 4% due on 1 April 2028, it’s a good time to check your fund, not just your contribution rate.

## Carry Out a Self-Check Against a KiwiSaver Statement

You can work out whether your KiwiSaver fund fits you in about two minutes, using your most recent annual statement.

Start by finding your fund type and provider name on the statement, then consider the questions below.

| **Factor**   | **Ask yourself**                                                                     | **If the answer's no**                                                  |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Timeline     | Does my fund's risk level match the number of years until I need my KiwiSaver money? | You might be carrying too much or too little risk for your timeframe    |
| Risk comfort | Would I be OK with watching this balance drop 10–20% in a bad year?                  | A lower-risk fund might suit you better, regardless of your timeline    |
| Values       | Am I comfortable with what this fund invests in?                                     | An ethical or values-based fund option might be a better option for you |
| Fees         | Do I know what I'm paying, and is it reasonable for what I get?                      | It’s worth comparing the fees with other providers                      |

Sorted’s guide [to picking the right fund](https://sorted.org.nz/guides/kiwisaver/kiwisaver-which-fund-suits/) makes an important point. It’s best not to base your decisions on last year's best-performing fund, as returns move around every year. Your timeline and risk comfort don't change nearly as often, so they're the more reliable things to plan around.

It can be tempting to treat “lowest fee” as shorthand for “best fund”, but it isn't always. A slightly higher fee can be worth it if the fund's performance, service or ethical screening suits you better.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know if I'm in the right KiwiSaver fund?

Check it against your goals, timeline, risk comfort and values, using the self-check table above. It’s a good idea to compare your options if your fund's risk level doesn't match how soon you need the money, or if you're not comfortable with what it invests in. Our [Fund Finder Quiz](https://bettersaver.co.nz/) does this comparison for you in a few minutes.

### What happens if I never actively choose a KiwiSaver fund?

You're placed in a default fund by your provider. It’s a balanced fund assigned automatically. This isn't a bad option, but it's not personalised to your specific timeline or goals.

### Can switching KiwiSaver funds cost me money?

Not normally. Switching is a transfer between providers, not a withdrawal, so there's typically no fee, no tax event and no loss of contributions. Some providers might apply small administration charges. Check your current provider's terms if you're unsure.

### How often should I review my KiwiSaver fund?

At least once a year, and any time your circumstances change (such as starting a new job, getting closer to buying a first home or approaching retirement). Checking your annual statement each year is a good habit.

### Is a more expensive KiwiSaver fund always a better one?

No. A higher fee can be worth it if the fund's performance, values-screening or service suit you better, but fees alone shouldn't be the deciding factor.

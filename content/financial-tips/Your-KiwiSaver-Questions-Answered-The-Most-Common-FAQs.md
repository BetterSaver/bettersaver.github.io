In this article, we answer the queries that we hear most often from people trying to make sense of their account, organised so you can jump straight to what you need.

We've also flagged two rules that changed recently, so you're working from what's current in 2026.

## Getting Started with KiwiSaver

Is KiwiSaver compulsory? Will I be automatically enrolled?

KiwiSaver isn't compulsory across the board, but [auto-enrolment applies when you start a new job.](https://www.ird.govt.nz/kiwisaver/kiwisaver-individuals/joining-kiwisaver/joining-kiwisaver-as-an-employee)

If you're a new employee taking on a permanent or full-time role (or a fixed-term contract of more than 28 days) and you're a New Zealand resident, your employer must automatically enrol you in KiwiSaver as part of your paperwork.

You then get a window between the end of week 2 and the end of week 8 from your start date to opt out, if you decide it isn't for you right now. Outside that scenario, joining is optional.

If you want the fuller picture of how contributions, funds and providers fit together, our [simple guide to how KiwiSaver works](https://bettersaver.co.nz/financial-tips/how-does-kiwisaver-work-a-simple-guide-for-2026/) is a good starting point.

## Can self-employed people or non-workers join KiwiSaver?

Anyone eligible [can join KiwiSaver directly](https://www.ird.govt.nz/kiwisaver/kiwisaver-individuals/joining-kiwisaver/joining-kiwisaver-if-im-self-employed-or-not-working) with a provider, including self-employed people, contractors and people who aren't currently working.

Auto-enrolment only ever happens through an employer, so if you fall into this group, you'll need to apply directly rather than wait for it to happen on its own. The main difference is that there's no employer matching your contributions.

You're still eligible for the annual government contribution, though, as long as you meet the minimum contribution threshold each year.

## Can I sign my kids up for KiwiSaver?

Children can join KiwiSaver, but [the process depends on their age](https://www.ird.govt.nz/kiwisaver/kiwisaver-individuals/joining-kiwisaver). For anyone under 16, a legal guardian needs to apply on the child's behalf.

For 16- and 17-year-olds, the young person can apply themselves, but a guardian still needs to co-sign the application.

Under-18s can't be automatically enrolled through an employer the way adults can. For this age group, joining always happens by applying directly, never automatically.

## Contributions & the Government Top-Up

### What is the government contribution, and how do I get it?

[The government contribution](https://www.ird.govt.nz/kiwisaver/kiwisaver-individuals/growing-my-kiwisaver-account/getting-the-kiwisaver-government-contribution) effectively adds 25 cents for every dollar you personally contribute to KiwiSaver, up to a maximum of $260.72 each year.

To receive the full amount, you need to have contributed at least $1,042.86 yourself between 1 July and 30 June, which IRD calls the "KiwiSaver year", for this calculation.

If you contribute less than that, you'll still receive 25c per dollar, just proportionally less. If you contribute more, the top-up caps out at $260.72 regardless.

It's available to members aged 16 to 65 with income under the relevant threshold, with 16- and 17-year-olds becoming eligible for the government contribution from 1 July 2025.

### How much do I have to contribute now?

If you haven't checked your contribution rate lately, the default minimum rate for employee and matching employer contributions [rose to 3.5% from 1 April 2026](https://www.ird.govt.nz/kiwisaver-changes), up from the previous 3%. It's set to rise again to 4% in 2028.

That's a meaningful shift if you've been sitting at the old default without reviewing it, so check your current rate against your payslip or provider statement.

For the full breakdown of what changed and what it means for your take-home pay, see our dedicated piece on [KiwiSaver Contribution Rates: What Changed in April 2026.](https://bettersaver.co.nz/financial-tips/kiwisaver-contribution-rates-what-changed-in-april-2026/)

And if you're still weighing up whether contributing is worth it in the first place, our article on 5 Reasons Why You Should Join KiwiSaver lays out the case in plain terms.

### Can I pause my KiwiSaver contributions?

[You can apply for a "savings suspension"](https://www.ird.govt.nz/kiwisaver/kiwisaver-individuals/making-changes-to-my-kiwisaver/taking-a-savings--break) at any time without giving a reason once you've been a KiwiSaver member for 12 months.

If you haven’t been a member for that long, you can still apply, but you'll need to show evidence of financial hardship for it to be approved.

Suspensions can last anywhere from three months to a full year, and you can renew one if you need it to run longer than that.

### What happens to my contributions when I turn 65?

You don't have to stop contributing at 65. You're welcome to keep going for as long as you like, if you're still earning and want to.

But [you stop qualifying for the government contribution once you're over 65](https://www.ird.govt.nz/kiwisaver/kiwisaver-individuals/getting-my-kiwisaver-when-i-retire), and depending on the terms of your individual employment agreement, your employer's contributions might also stop at that point rather than continuing automatically.

It's worth checking your specific employment agreement if this applies to you, since arrangements vary from employer to employer.

## Switching & Providers

### Can I switch KiwiSaver providers at any time?

[You can switch KiwiSaver providers](https://www.ird.govt.nz/kiwisaver/kiwisaver-individuals/making-changes-to-my-kiwisaver/changing-to-another-provider) at any time and for any reason. There's no lock-in period or minimum term you need to wait out first.

You can only be with one KiwiSaver scheme at a time, so switching means moving your account from one provider to another, not adding a second provider alongside your existing one.

### Do I need to tell my employer if I switch?

You don't need to tell your employer when you switch providers. Once your new provider is confirmed with Inland Revenue, your employer's contributions continue as normal and are simply redirected to your new scheme, with no extra paperwork on your part.

For a full step-by-step walkthrough of the process, see our guide on [How to Change Your KiwiSaver Provider.](https://bettersaver.co.nz/pages/how-to-change-your-kiwisaver-provider)

### How long does switching take?

According to Inland Revenue, the [transfer process typically takes around two weeks](https://www.ird.govt.nz/kiwisaver/kiwisaver-individuals/making-changes-to-my-kiwisaver/changing-to-another-provider) from start to finish once you've applied with your new provider. Some providers charge a transfer fee, so confirm this with both your current and prospective provider before you commit.

### Can I be with more than one KiwiSaver provider at once?

Under current rules, [you can only belong to one KiwiSaver scheme at a time](https://www.ird.govt.nz/kiwisaver/kiwisaver-individuals/making-changes-to-my-kiwisaver/changing-to-another-provider). If you're hoping to spread your savings across multiple providers or fund types, that isn't possible within KiwiSaver itself.

You'll need to choose a single provider, which may offer a combination of funds within its own scheme, rather than holding accounts with several providers side by side.

## Using Your KiwiSaver

### Can I use my KiwiSaver to buy my first home?

Most members [can withdraw some of their KiwiSaver savings](https://www.ird.govt.nz/kiwisaver/kiwisaver-individuals/getting-my-kiwisaver-funds-early/getting-my-kiwisaver-for-my-first-home) to help buy a first home once they've been contributing for at least three years, provided they leave a minimum balance in their account.

Eligibility criteria are specific to your own situation, though, so rather than assume you qualify, contact your provider directly.

### Can I withdraw early because of significant financial hardship?

If you're facing significant financial hardship, such as being unable to meet minimum everyday living costs, [you may be able to apply for an early withdrawal.](https://www.ird.govt.nz/kiwisaver/kiwisaver-individuals/getting-my-kiwisaver-funds-early/getting-my-kiwisaver-funds-for-significant-financial-hardship)

Your own contributions, any employer contributions and investment earnings on your account can be withdrawn under this rule. The government contribution isn’t included.

Applications go through your provider, who assesses each case against Inland Revenue's hardship criteria.

### What happens to my KiwiSaver if I die?

Your KiwiSaver savings become part of your estate, and the executor or administrator contacts the provider with the death certificate and proof of authority. The balance is then paid to the estate.

Exactly how the funds are distributed depends on your circumstances (whether you have a will, who your beneficiaries are and how your estate is administered).

This is a case where contacting your provider directly for guidance is the right next step, rather than assuming a single process applies to everyone.
